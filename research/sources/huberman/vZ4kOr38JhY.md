# AMA #2: Improve Sleep, Reduce Sugar Cravings, Optimal Protein Intake, Stretching Frequency & More
- Guest: Huberman solo (Premium AMA episode)

## What deep sleep (slow-wave sleep) is
- Deep sleep / slow-wave sleep (SWS) predominates in the first half of the night; REM sleep becomes more prevalent in the second half — this is the typical pattern absent disruptors like caffeine/alcohol.
- Growth hormone secretion happens every night but is mostly concentrated in the first half of sleep (i.e., during SWS-dominant hours); growth hormone supports metabolism, protein synthesis, and tissue/muscle repair.
- Dreams during SWS tend to be more mundane/less emotionally charged than REM dreams (which carry more emotional content and are theorized to support a kind of overnight "trauma release," aided by REM-associated body paralysis/atonia).
- Cited recent research (breath and blood-draw monitoring of sleeping subjects in a lab): the body metabolizes energy differently in the first half of the night (SWS-dominant) vs. the second half (REM-dominant) — relevant to how the body regulates insulin and blood glucose.

## Sleep-stage balance and tracker accuracy
- General goal: balance total SWS and REM across the night, though Huberman cautions against obsessing over exact percentages (e.g., not literally needing 30%/30%).
- Consumer sleep trackers (Whoop, Oura, Eight Sleep, mattress-based trackers) estimate sleep stage using heart rate, heart rate variability, and movement (using REM's characteristic muscle paralysis/atonia as a proxy) — these are "best guesses," not the EEG/EMG-based direct measurement used in dedicated sleep labs (e.g., Stanford, University of Pennsylvania).

## Don'ts for maximizing slow-wave sleep
- Avoid alcohol within 8 hours of bedtime (or at all) — even a single lunchtime drink can reduce SWS/REM and disrupt overall sleep architecture in some people.
- Avoid caffeine within 8, and ideally within 12, hours of bedtime.
- Avoid cannabis, THC, and CBD within 8–12 hours of sleep — referencing discussion with Matt Walker (UC Berkeley professor of neuroscience/psychology) that alcohol, cannabis, and CBD can all help sleep onset while still degrading overall sleep quality/architecture.
- Avoid eating within about 2 hours of bedtime to maximize growth hormone output and SWS depth/duration — but not to the point of being too hungry to fall asleep (that also disrupts sleep). Huberman's own pattern: dinner 6–8 p.m., sleep 10–11 p.m. (roughly a 2-hour buffer); a small snack is acceptable if genuinely hungry near bedtime.

## Exercise and slow-wave sleep
- Cited study: "Exercise Improves the Quality of Slow Wave Sleep by Increasing Slow Wave Stability" — a randomized crossover trial.
- Protocol used: 60 minutes of exercise at 60% of VO2 max (roughly zone 3–4 cardio intensity — harder than a conversational pace), performed at least 6 hours before bedtime, with caffeine and alcohol abstained from in all study conditions.
- Result: measurable enhancement of slow-wave sleep quality (via EEG "power"/fine brain-wave structure), despite no significant change in core body temperature and no strong subjective improvement reported by the exercising subjects.
- Exercising intensely within 6 hours of bedtime can disrupt both SWS and REM.
- No study, per Huberman at time of recording, has specifically tested resistance training's effect on SWS, though he speculates a similar benefit likely applies if done more than 6 hours before sleep.

## Supplement stack for sleep
- Huberman's personal nightly stack (taken 30–60 minutes before sleep): magnesium threonate (or interchangeably magnesium bisglycinate — both cross into cells and the blood-brain barrier effectively) + L-theanine + apigenin (the calming compound found in chamomile).
- Caution: theanine may worsen vivid dreaming, night terrors, or sleepwalking in susceptible individuals.
- Melatonin: not recommended by Huberman — commercial supplements frequently mislabel actual melatonin content, and melatonin affects other hormone systems beyond sleep.
- Myo-inositol: cited study — "The impact of myo-inositol supplementation on sleep quality in pregnant women, a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled study" — showed improved sleep quality and architecture. Huberman personally takes 900 mg of myo-inositol (added to the stack above), especially when following a lower-carbohydrate diet or when hungry near bedtime; reports easier return to sleep after nighttime waking. Myo-inositol is also studied for anxiety reduction and fertility support (particularly in women).

## REM sleep deprivation and sleep amount
- REM deprivation across multiple nights is linked to reduced next-day emotional regulation ability, though some depression treatments deliberately use REM deprivation (referenced a separate episode with Dr. Nolan Williams for detail).
- No specific study is known (per Huberman, at time of recording) directly linking a low SWS:REM ratio to negative health outcomes, though he reasons reduced SWS likely impairs tissue/exercise recovery given growth hormone's role.
- Total sleep need decreases with age (children and those going through puberty need more sleep than adults).
- Best simple marker of adequate nighttime sleep: whether you're sleepy during the day. Needing an afternoon nap (Huberman naps 20–30 minutes) doesn't necessarily indicate insufficient nighttime sleep.
- General sleep-duration guidance: at least 6 hours per night for roughly 95% of people, ideally closer to 7–8 hours. Huberman personally reports feeling well-rested on 6.5–7 hours plus an occasional 20–30 minute nap or NSDR (non-sleep deep rest) session.
